## 1. Schedule EIC (Form 1040) — current-year form

**Official title (verbatim from the form header):** "SCHEDULE EIC (Form 1040) 2025 — Earned Income Credit — Qualifying Child Information"
**Issuer line, verbatim:** "Department of the Treasury / Internal Revenue Service"
**Direct PDF URL:** https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040sei.pdf
**About page:** https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-eic-form-1040
**OMB No. 1545-0074 · Attachment Sequence No. 43 · Cat. No. 13339M · footer reads "Schedule EIC (Form 1040) 2025 Created 11/17/25"**

Tax year 2025 is the current-year Schedule EIC as of 2026-09-13 (it is the form for returns filed in the 2026 filing season). No 2026 Schedule EIC was published at that URL on this date.

Subtitle instructions, verbatim:

> "Complete and attach to Form 1040 or 1040-SR only if you have a qualifying child."
> "Go to www.irs.gov/ScheduleEIC for the latest information."

Header fields, verbatim: "Name(s) shown on return" · "Your social security number"

### "Before you begin:" (verbatim)

> "• See the instructions for Form 1040, line 27a, to make sure that (a) you can take the EIC, and (b) you have a qualifying child. See also Pub. 596.
> • Be sure the child's name on line 1 and social security number (SSN) on line 2 agree with the child's social security card. Otherwise, at the time we process your return, we may reduce your EIC. If the name or SSN on the child's social security card is not correct, call the Social Security Administration at 800-772-1213.
> • If you have a child who meets the conditions to be your qualifying child for purposes of claiming the EIC, but that child doesn't have an SSN as defined in the instructions for Form 1040, line 27a, see the instructions."

### CAUTION block (verbatim)

> "• You can't claim the EIC for a child who didn't live with you for more than half of the year.
> • If your child doesn't have an SSN as defined in the instructions for Form 1040, line 27a, see the instructions.
> • If you take the EIC even though you are not eligible, you may not be allowed to take the credit for up to 10 years. See the instructions for details.
> • It will take us longer to process your return and issue your refund if you do not fill in all lines that apply for each qualifying child."

### Section: "Qualifying Child Information" — columns "Child 1", "Child 2", "Child 3"

All field labels below are verbatim from the form. The same six lines repeat across the three child columns.

**1 — "Child's name"**
Sub-instruction: "If you have more than three qualifying children, you have to list only three to get the maximum credit."
Entry sub-labels: "First name" · "Last name"

**2 — "Child's SSN"**
Sub-instruction: "The child must have an SSN as defined in the instructions for Form 1040, line 27a, unless the child was born and died in 2025 or you are claiming the self-only EIC (see instructions). If your child was born and died in 2025 and did not have an SSN, enter "Died" on this line and attach a copy of the child's birth certificate, death certificate, or hospital medical records showing a live birth."

**3 — "Child's year of birth"**
Entry sub-label: "Year"
Sub-instruction: "If born after 2006 and the child is younger than you (or your spouse if filing jointly), skip lines 4a and 4b; go to line 5."

**4a — "Was the child under age 24 at the end of 2025, a student, and younger than you (or your spouse if filing jointly)?"**
Checkboxes: "Yes. Go to line 5." · "No. Go to line 4b."

**4b (printed as "b") — "Was the child permanently and totally disabled during any part of 2025?"**
Checkboxes: "Yes. Go to line 5." · "No. The child is not a qualifying child."

**5 — "Child's relationship to you"**
Sub-instruction: "(for example, son, daughter, grandchild, niece, nephew, eligible foster child, etc.)"

**6 — "Number of months child lived with you in the United States during 2025"**
Sub-instructions: "• If the child lived with you for more than half of 2025 but less than 7 months, enter "7."" · "• If the child was born or died in 2025 and your home was the child's home for more than half the time they were alive during 2025, enter "12.""
Entry sub-labels: "months" · "Do not enter more than 12 months."

Footer, verbatim: "For Paperwork Reduction Act Notice, see your tax return instructions."

### Page 2 of the form — "Qualifying Child" flowchart (verbatim)

> "A qualifying child for the EIC is your...
> Son, daughter, stepchild, eligible foster child, brother, sister, stepbrother, stepsister, half brother, half sister, or a descendant of any of them (for example, your grandchild, niece, or nephew)
> AND was...
> Under age 19 at the end of 2025 and younger than you (or your spouse if filing jointly)
> or
> Under age 24 at the end of 2025, a student, and younger than you (or your spouse if filing jointly)
> or
> Any age and permanently and totally disabled
> AND
> Who is not filing a joint return for 2025 or is filing a joint return for 2025 only to claim a refund of withheld income tax or estimated tax paid
> AND
> Who lived with you in the United States for more than half of 2025."

Page 2 CAUTION, verbatim:

> "You can't claim the EIC for a child who didn't live with you for more than half of the year, even if you paid most of the child's living expenses. The IRS may ask you for documents to show you lived with each qualifying child. Documents you might want to keep for this purpose include school and childcare records and other records that show your child's address."

## 2. IRS "Qualifying child rules" page — the four tests

Page title, verbatim: **"Qualifying child rules"**
URL: https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/earned-income-tax-credit/qualifying-child-rules

**Relationship** — verbatim: "Your child must be your: Son, daughter, stepchild, adopted child or foster child; Brother, sister, half-brother, half-sister, stepsister or stepbrother; Grandchild, niece or nephew"

**Age** — verbatim, any one of:
- "Under age 19 at the end of the year **and** younger than you (or your spouse, if you file a joint return)"
- "Under age 24 at the end of the year **and** a full-time student for at least 5 months of the year and younger than you (or your spouse, if you file a joint return)"
- "Any age **and** permanently and totally disabled at any time during the year"

**Residency** — verbatim: "Your child must live in the same home as you in the United States for more than half of the tax year."

**Joint return** — verbatim: "Your child must not have filed a joint return with another person (for example, their husband or wife) to claim any credits such as the EITC."

## 3. EITC income limits and maximum credit — tax year 2025

Sources (both irs.gov, mutually corroborating):
- "Earned income and Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) tables" — https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/earned-income-tax-credit/earned-income-and-earned-income-tax-credit-eitc-tables
- Publication 596 (2025), "Earned Income Credit (EIC), For use in preparing 2025 Returns", file dated 18-Nov-2025 — https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p596.pdf

### AGI / earned income limits (tax year 2025)

| Children or relatives claimed | Single, head of household, or married filing separately | Married filing jointly |
|---|---|---|
| Zero | $19,104 | $26,214 |
| One | $50,434 | $57,554 |
| Two | $57,310 | $64,430 |
| Three or more | $61,555 | $68,675 |

Pub 596, Rule 1, verbatim:

> "Rule 1—Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) Limits
> Your adjusted gross income (AGI) must be less than:
> • $61,555 ($68,675 for married filing jointly) if you have three or more qualifying children who have valid SSNs,
> • $57,310 ($64,430 for married filing jointly) if you have two qualifying children who have valid SSNs,
> • $50,434 ($57,554 for married filing jointly) if you have one qualifying child who has a valid SSN, or
> • $19,104 ($26,214 for married filing jointly) if you don't have a qualifying child who has a valid SSN."

### Investment income limit (tax year 2025)

Verbatim from the tables page: "$11,950 or less". Pub 596 Rule 6 title, verbatim: "Your Investment Income Must Be $11,950 or Less."

### Maximum credit amounts (tax year 2025)

| Qualifying children | Maximum credit |
|---|---|
| No qualifying children | $649 |
| 1 qualifying child | $4,328 |
| 2 qualifying children | $7,152 |
| 3 or more qualifying children | $8,046 |

Source: the tables page above. All four figures also appear in Publication 596 (2025) — $649 and $4,328 as the column ceilings in the EIC table, $7,152 and $8,046 likewise — so the row is double-sourced on irs.gov.

## 4. EITC participation rate (for SOURCES.md)

Four irs.gov items, strongest verbatim first. **Note the noun: IRS writes "eligible workers" in the outreach article and "eligible taxpayers" in the 2026 EITC Central page. Quote whichever you cite; do not swap the word.**

### 4.1 Exact "four out of five" sentence (verbatim)

> "The IRS estimates that four out of five eligible workers claim and get their EITC."

- **Page title:** "Sample article for organizations, employers and others to use to reach customers (244 word count)"; the article inside is headed "Tell one person about the Earned Income Tax Credit"
- **URL:** https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-utl/OC-EITCTellOnePerson.pdf
- **Date:** IRS Outreach Corner sample article tied to "EITC Awareness Day – January 29, 2016" (PDF created 2016-01-06). **This is the oldest of the four items; its dollar figures in the same paragraph ($6,242 / $53,267) are 2015 tax year and must not be reused.** The participation sentence itself is what is being cited.

### 4.2 Current-dated IRS statement of the same fact (verbatim)

> "Four out of five eligible taxpayers receive the EITC."

- **Page title:** "50 years of Earned Income Tax Credit" (EITC Central)
- **URL:** https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/eitc-central/50-years-of-earned-income-tax-credit
- **Date:** "Page Last Reviewed or Updated: 18-Aug-2026"
- This page is the one to cite if the pitch needs a **currently dated** IRS source for "four out of five".

### 4.3 The complement, from the 2026 EITC Awareness Day release (verbatim)

> "The IRS estimates that about one in five EITC-eligible taxpayers don't claim this valuable credit, underscoring the importance of the annual EITC Awareness Day outreach campaign."

- **Page title:** "IRS joins national partners to launch EITC Awareness Day"
- **Release:** IR-2026-11
- **Date:** Jan. 23, 2026
- **URL:** https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-joins-national-partners-to-launch-eitc-awareness-day

### 4.4 The measured rate behind the claim

- **Page title:** "EITC participation rate by state"
- **URL:** https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/eitc-central/eitc-participation-rate-by-state
- **Date:** "Page Last Reviewed or Updated: 19-Aug-2026"
- **National participation rate, tax year 2022: 80.8%.**
- Definition, verbatim: "The percentage of eligible taxpayers who receive an EITC payment (number receiving an EITC payment/Number eligible for EITC)."

Related nationwide totals, from "EITC reports and statistics" (https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/earned-income-tax-credit/eitc-reports-and-statistics, updated 22-Jan-2026): as of December 2025, about 24 million eligible workers and families received about $70 billion in EITC; the average EITC for tax year 2024 was about $2,894.

### Not usable (recorded so nobody re-finds it and mistakes it)

- The release "IRS joins national partners to launch 20th Annual Earned Income Tax Credit Awareness Day" (DC-2026-01, Jan. 23, 2026) states "One in five eligible taxpayers in the District of Columbia don't claim this credit worth up to $8,046". That is a **District of Columbia** figure, not national.
- The 2019 release "EITC Awareness Day reminds taxpayers to look into valuable credit; Volunteers available to help" contains "Four out of five people who get refunds now choose the speed and convenience of direct deposit" — that is about **direct deposit**, not EITC participation. Do not cite it for participation.
